A deep learning-based algorithm for online detection of small target defects in large-size sawn timber


Min Ji , Wei Zhang *, Jia-kai Han , Hu Miao , Xing-liang Diao, Guo-fu Wang


Research Institute of Wood Industry, Chinese Academy of Forestry, Beijing 100091, China


## A R T I C L E I N F O


## A B S T R A C T


Keywords: Deep learning YOLO training model Efficient layer aggregation network


As a core material in wood structure buildings, the surface quality and grade of timber are crucial to the safety of these structures. The objectives of this study are a) to develop an online detection system for small target defects in large-size Pinus densiflora sawn timber using machine vision/deep learning technology; b) to enhance timber productivity and quality by efficiently and accurately detecting defects using predictive models generated by a deep learning-based algorithm. The predictive models generated by deep learning with a YOLO-integrated network structure are utilized in this study. The proposed methods, including image stitching, segmentation, and fusion techniques based on SIFT features, enable the input of large-size sawn timber oversize images while preserving the integrity of the information. The efficient layer aggregation network enhances machine vision defect detection on timber sawing lines, adapting to variable environments with a focus on Pinus densiflora timber. The results indicated that the machine vision defect detection device is able to predict candidate bounding boxes and class probabilities for multiple types of knots in complex, naturally characterized materials, even under conditions of background noise and interfering factors. Comparing the detection results of the proposed system with the statistical outcomes of manual visual inspections under production conditions involving long hours and large quantities of sawn timber yielded an identification and detection accuracy of 90.37 %. The system’s speed for detecting knot defects on the surface of sawn timber can reach 40 m/min, making it suitable for practical application of wood product processing lines.


## 1. Introduction


Pinus densiflora sawn timber is a common biomass energy feedstock, popular for its high fiber content, good mechanical properties and durability. Detecting and grading wood is a crucial step in determining its suitability and quality, which are closely linked to its effectiveness and sustainability as a biomass building material. Precise grading and detection can encourage the widespread use of biomass materials and enhance the value of wood resources.


Visual grading is the traditional method of grading sawn timber based on the visual assessment of its properties. The mechanical prop­ erties of defective sawn timber differ from those of defect-free timber. In general, the mechanical properties of sawn timber are affected by growth characteristics, which can be judged visually (Hyeusoo et al., 2021). Therefore, the principle of the visual grading process of sawn timber relies on the premise of property evaluation.


Knots significantly affect the mechanical properties of structural sawn timber and are considered a typical defect. Knots with high texture

The texture around the knots is complex and irregular, which af­ fects the uniformity and consistency of the sawn timber. The characteristics of knots affect their own mechanical properties, and their physical properties, such as texture and density, differ from those of normal wood. During drying, cracks easily form around knots, and dead knots may fall off, significantly reducing the echanical properties and later application value of structural sawn timber (Barmpoutis et al., 2018). Currently, factories primarily use manual visual grading, a tradi­ tional grading method that relies on human experience and observa­ tional ability. However, the visual grading has several drawbacks: it is highly subjective and inefficient. The accuracy of manual visual grading is limited by the observer’s ability and experience, potentially falling short of the desired precision. Factors such as lighting and background conditions can also impact the effectiveness and accuracy of visual grading. Due to the subjectivity and uncertainty associated with manual

visual grading, achieving standardization and consistency is chal­ lenging. Consequently, more enterprises and research efforts have explored the use of machine vision technology for automated grading to address these issues (Bengio et al., 2021).


With the continuous advancement of computer vision technology, sawn timber detection systems urgently need to adopt more advanced image processing and recognition technologies, such as deep learning, neural networks, to enhance accuracy and reliability (Li et al., 2023). The sawn timber detection system will further enhance automation, reduce manual intervention, and improve both production efficiency and timber quality. Machine vision technology will automatically identify and reject defective wood, eliminating the need for manual inspection (Dong et al., 2021). To deal with the complex production environment and the changing requirements of sawn timber grading, the sawn timber inspection system will be more adaptable, automatically adjusting parameters and optimizing performance according to actual conditions (Niu et al., 2021). Machine vision inspection technology provides a more comprehensive wood quality management technology for wood classification, grading, pricing, etc. (Ghosh et al., 2019). In the future, the wood machine vision detection system will form a closer cooperative relationship with human operators and jointly complete complex detection tasks through human-machine collaboration, to improve work efficiency and quality (Ganaie et al., 2022). In general, the development trend of machine vision in sawn timber detection is to develop in the direction of higher precision, higher automation, stronger adaptability, wider application, and closer human-machine collabora­ tion (Dargan et al., 2020).


Knots can be categorized based on factors such as their cause, morphology, and impact. Knots vary in shape, size, and form, and are randomly distribute in different positions and directions. The complex variation in shape, texture, distribution, and color makes the anisotropy of sawn timber more pronounced. The above reasons make machine vision inspection more difficult (Gu et al., 2018). In a real production environment, lighting conditions and environmental factors are often uncertain, which can have an impact on the quality of the captured images, which affects the accuracy of defect detection. For example, bright light or shadows can over- or under-expose certain areas in the image, making it impossible to identify defects. Background noise, mutual occlusion between products, and a variety of interfering factors such as scratches, stains, uneven color, etc., can interfere with detection. How to eliminate these interferences and improve the detection accu­ racy is another challenge faced by machine vision detection (Kamilaris and Prenafeta-Boldú., 2018; Gawlikowski et al., 2023). As a production line is upgraded or a product changes, detection algorithms need to be able to adapt to new situations (Cong et al., 2019; Zuo et al., 2022). This requires algorithms to be scalable and adaptive, able to self-adjust and learn from new data and environmental conditions (Bartlett et al., 2021; Choudhary et al., 2022). The complexity of the task of machine vision-based sawn timber detection arises from several factors, including the large amount of data, slow processing speed, diversity of defect types, the influence of light and environmental changes, background noise and interference, as well as the scalability and adaptability of algorithms.


Traditional methods like grayscale, binary filtering, edge detection, and morphological processing are sensitive to environmental changes, requiring manual intervention. These methods lack generalization, involve heavy computation, are highly sensitive to parameter settings, and are incapable of processing large-scale data or capturing advanced image features (Pang et al., 2021; Shwartz and Armon., 2022; Abdar et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2021). Therefore, a solution to these challenges is necessary. Deep learning is widely used for various detection tasks, particularly in image recognition, and its superior performance has been recognized (Wright et al., 2022; Paul and Chung, 2018; Jiang et al., 2021). Deep learning algorithms such as Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), Recurrent Neural Network (RNN), YOLO (You Only Look Once), Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), U-Net, VGGNet have their


own advantages and application scenarios, and it is necessary to select the appropriate algorithm according to the specific task requirements (Chung et al., 2021; Kattenborn et al., 2021; Sun et al., 2021; Bejani and Ghatee., 2021). The YOLO algorithm has the following advantages in defect detection: rapidity, accuracy, versatility, etc. (Liu et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 2021). Therefore, it has become one of the most popular detection methods in wood detection and processing. The YOLO deep learning method, trained on a large dataset, can automatically extract features from images and classify or regress them according to these features. Compared with other methods, deep learning offers higher detection accuracy and robustness.


As a machine learning structure model, deep learning has significant advantages in solving high-dimensional data. The deep learning network model adopts an end-to-end, multi-hidden layer, layer-by-layer learning method, and its working principle is to imitate the thinking characteristics of the human brain, mining and learning data features from shallow to deep, and is more suitable for big data, data feature abstraction, and complexity (Sun et al., 2024; Kiranyaz et al., 2021). Currently, the most used deep learning models use multi-layer neural networks, where each layer of neural networks performs a nonlinear mapping of the inputs. Through this process, deep neural networks can analyze and compute more abstract features, enabling the detection and classification of complex sawn timber defects (Chen et al., 2024; Pouyanfar et al., 2018).


In summary, manual visual grading faces challenges such as wood anisotropy, the complexity of production line visual tasks, large-size sawn timber images, and small target detection defects. When applying machine vision nondestructive testing technology to optimize the processing of Pinus densiflora structural sawn timber, many tech­ nical issues must be addressed. In this approach, to apply machine vision nondestructive testing technology for optimizing the processing of structural sawn timber, several technical challenges were addressed. The most critical issues were the accuracy and speed of the identifying knots on the surface of sawn timber. The accuracy of defect identifica­ tion for sawn timber knots directly impacted the quality of sawn timber processed products, while the identification speed was closely related to production efficiency and profitability. To meet the practical re­ quirements of structural sawn timber in the processing line of wood products, the intelligent detection system for knot defects for long-size Pinus densiflora structural sawn timber was designed and developed. This work accelerated the transformation of the traditional manufacturing industry into digital, networked, and intelligent, pro­ moted the extension of the industrial chain to the upstream and down­ stream, and formed a more complete industrial chain and industrial clusters.


This study primarily addressed five issues in the timber grading process: (1) Low accuracy and efficiency of manual visual grading, along with high labor intensity; (2) The complexity of knots in terms of type, shape, texture, distribution, and color variation; (3) Poor adaptability and flexibility in handling complex visual tasks on the sawn timber production line; (4) Limitations in processing large-size sawn timber images, leading to information loss; (5) Challenges in deep learning small target detection, where background noise and image interference complicate classification and positioning accuracy. The research method was aimed at achieving intelligent inspection and statistic results of sawn timber surfaces, thereby improving processing efficiency and meeting the requirements of practical applications in timber production lines. The machine vision inspection system significantly improved the inspection efficiency and speed on the production line through auto­ mated image processing and analysis capabilities. It ensures product quality consistency and production process continuity, contributing to the sustainable development of biomass materials.

## 2. Materials and methods


## 2.1. Materials


Pinus densiflora dimension lumber is widely used for building appli­ cations where the dimensional stability, natural durability, fine appearance, material quality, and building safety are highly required. In this study, a new algorithm was proposed to measure the size of the lumber, and the errors, accuracy, and effectiveness of size measurement were analyzed. In the experiment, the lumber size was approximately 3000–6000 mm×150 mm×42 mm, and the moisture content of the tested dimension lumber was between 12 % and 15 %. The factory application experiment was completed at Suzhou Crownhomes Co., LTD., Suzhou city, Jiangsu Province, China. The environment temper­ ature of that day was 28◦C and relative humidity was 63 % (Fig. 1).


## 2.2. Image acquisition


Based on deep learning, surface knots the structural sawn timber were identified and evaluated by, and the structural sawn timber surface image acquisition device was built to obtain the defect images. The structural sawn timber surface image acquisition platform and func­ tional module logic diagram were built and are shown in Fig. 2. To enhance the functionality and maintenance of the image acquisition platform, a modular design approach was employed in the development of the structural sawn timber surface image acquisition platform. The image acquisition platform was mainly composed of three parts: struc­ tural sawn timber transmission module, image acquisition module, and sensing control module.


The image acquisition platform selected a Basler raL2048–48gm line scan camera to collect pictures of structural sawn timber surface defects with a horizontal/vertical resolution of 2048×1024 pixels, exposure time of 13 s, bandwidth of 861.8 Mbps, and line rate of 52083.33 Hz. When the image height was 1024 pixels, the resulting frame rate was 50.86 fps. To meet the needs of sample training of deep learning algo­ rithms, sufficient images of different types of knot defects on the sawn timber surface were collected. The working parameters of the image processing center were: processor: 12th Gen IntelR Core(TM) i9–12900 K 3.20 GHz, with RAM 64.0 GB, system type was 64-bit operating system, based on x64 processor, Windows 11 system, GPU model: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090, and the GPU memory was 55.8 GB.


## 2.3. Knot samples


When training and testing the sawn timber, different types of knots (mainly including dead knots and sound knots, sound knots are divided


into round knot, spike knot, and edge knot) were selected for training and testing (Table 1). 17885 defect images were collected, including 4556 images of sawn timber without defects, 2868 images of dead knots, 4268 images of round knots, 3169 images of spike knots, and 3024 images of edge knots. 80 % of these images were randomly selected as training collections, 20 % as validation collections, and the test image collections had 14400 images. First, the 4800 prediction image collec­ tions were detected, and if there was a misjudgment of the knot defect, the misjudgment of the knot images would be added to the training collections and re-trained until the model training reached a good detection accuracy.


2.4. Machine vision defect detection of knots on large-size structural sawn timber


A batch of 3000–6000 mm large-size sawn timber was selected for the observation by machine vision processing operations. As shown in Fig. 3, the method of detecting and identifying knots on the surface of large-size sawn timber based on deep learning image features mainly included the average segmentation processing of the original large-size image (the size of the split image was 640 pixel×640 pixel), the feature recognition of the deep learning algorithm model, and the knots size calculation.


For the large-size sawn timber surface defect detection method based on the YOLO algorithm, after detecting defects in the small-size knot image, the image was evenly segmented and then needed to be reinte­ grated into the large-size sawn timber image. The image stitching method based on SIFT features was used to stitch and fuse the segmented images. The position coordinates (x,y) and size (h,w) of the knot defect image were obtained in the image segmentation part, and the image information of knot defect on the surface of large-size sawn timber was obtained based on the image ROI = image (cv::Rect (x,y)) in OpenCVPython.

2.5.2. Efficient layer aggregation network The detection system considers the model design from the perspec­ tive of gradient optimization, which can enable the network to obtain better and more stable performance with fewer parameters. A highefficiency layer aggregation network (ELAN) used a stack structure in computational blocks to optimize the gradient length of the entire network. ELAN mainly solved the problem of poor performance in model scaling of deep learning YOLO models (Fig. 5). The CSPNet networking framework was designed to enhance online learning and accelerate inference, and combined the concept of gradient composition with hardware utilization efficiency. CSPNet combined channel split and cross stage connection to accomplish preset goals without changing the original network computing unit (Fig. 6).


2.5.3. Precision and error evaluation of training model The PR curve is the curve of precision and recall. From the perspective of prediction results, detection precision = TP/(TP+FP), TP+FP represents all data that is predicted to be positive samples, and TP represents the number of positive samples that are correctly pre­ dicted. Both recall and TPR (true positive rate) are counted from the real sample set, indicating how many positive samples the model retrieved out of the total positive samples. Recall/TPR= TP/(TP+FN), FN means that the predicted value is different from the true value, and the pre­ dicted value is a negative sample. When the PR curve is drawn, different classification thresholds are set to obtain the corresponding coordinates. The performance of the model using a PR curve was evaluated. ## 3. Results and discussions


## 3.1. Large-size sawn timber knot detection results based on deep learning


Through the knot defect detection of sawn timber by the above methods, the defects of knots on the surface of sawn timber can be accurately located and identified. The statistical results of 100 sawn timbers (five groups) are shown in Table 2. Based on the deep learning knot detection method, the identification and detection accuracy of knot defects on the surface of sawn timber can reach 90.37 %. The system meets the practical requirements of sawn timber in the production line of wooden products.


## 3.2. Statistics of knots position and areas


Taking the test sample No. 12–1-A as an example, the total number of defects can be obtained, and the information of the area, perimeter, barycenter X, barycenter Y, angle, long axis, short axis, circularity, and rectangularity of each node were obtained (Table 3). ## 3.3. Analysis of algorithm detection accuracy and efficiency


The analysis of the evaluation results of the knot defect dataset for structural sawn timber revealed that the image collection rate was 4.2 fps (with image heights of 12288 pixels). The time spent on image input, pre-processing (including image normalization), network upload time,


post-processing time (non-maximum suppression), and output results totaled 3 fps, resulting in a sawn timber detection speed of 1.2 m per second. Excluding the influence of hardware transmission rate, the detection speed of the image processed using the algorithm was 4.0 fps. The higher the confidence level, the more accurate the category detec­ tion was. The results of the system were compared with the statistical results of manual visual inspection, and the detection accuracy was 90.37 %, as shown in Table 4. Based on the developed system, the sawn timber was quickly scan­ ned, and images were processed online, and the intelligent detection and digital statistics of the sawn timber surface defects were completed, so as to improve the processing efficiency of structural sawn timber. It was focused on solving the problem of accuracy and speed of structural sawn timber in machine vision nondestructive testing. The system met the practical application of sawn timber in the production line of wood products. It was summarized as: (1) The detection method can achieve a maximum width of 220 mm for sawn timber; (2) The method can offer a maximum detection width of 6000 mm for sawn timber; (3) The detection speed of knots on the surface of sawn timber can reach 40 m/ min; (4) The detection accuracy of knots on the surface of sawn timber can reach more than 90.37 %.


As indicated in Table 5, the trio of methods were used to measure the sawn timber during the test and the efficiency and accuracy of detection were evaluated. The target object’s color change (preprocessing) tech­ nique yielded a 75.12 % detection accuracy and a 3.2-second detection time for knot. The deep learning approach yields a 90.37 % detection accuracy and 3.1-second detection time for knots. The approach demonstrated excellent adaptive capacity and robustness during testing, together with good detection efficiency and accuracy. The manual measurement approach, which gathers data by visual experiments, is inefficient and heavily subjective. According to the actual application, the deep learning method reduces subjectivity and improves accuracy and efficiency of detection, making it more appropriate for large-scale sawn timber inspection.
